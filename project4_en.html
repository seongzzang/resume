Binder
'BINDER' is an all-in-one service for tutors to manage their students, aiming to let everyone involved in tutoring use the features they need in one place. Built with UIKit and the MVC pattern, I implemented a custom calendar for schedule coordination. I also built a flexible data management structure using MongoDB.

Resolving Team Conflict over Late-Stage Design Changes
Problem Identification
A few days before development wrapped up, the design was changed across the board without considering the development team's progress, causing conflict between the development and design teams. The development team had been accommodating frequent design changes all along, which the design team took to mean the schedule was not an issue, while the development team felt burdened by the sudden large-scale changes. Because we were working remotely and communicating mostly in writing, each side's situation and intentions were not conveyed accurately.
Problem Solution
Sensing that misunderstandings were building up, I proposed an in-person meeting to both teams and coordinated the schedule. Talking face to face, we confirmed that the conflict stemmed from a lack of communication that each side had assumed was fine, and by sharing our situations and opinions directly, we were able to clear up the misunderstandings. Through this process, I learned how important it is to share development schedules and the scope of changes transparently across teams, and to act as a bridge who relays opinions and sets up discussions. Since then, when working with designers and planners, I have made a habit of sharing upfront how changes will affect development.
Choosing a Design Pattern and Restructuring MVC for Refactoring
Problem Identification
The app had many structural limitations because it was built early on, when my Swift knowledge was limited and I relied heavily on searching, so I began refactoring to improve it. The existing code was Storyboard-based, and although it was split into classes, their roles were not clearly separated, so it was hard to say the MVC pattern had been properly applied. The key question was which design pattern would give the app the right structure.
Problem Solution
First, I reviewed the major design patterns and compared which one fit the app's current scale and situation. Then, judging that a precise understanding of MVC's separation of roles had to come before adopting MVVM, I carried out an MVC refactoring that converted the Storyboard-based structure to code-based UI and clearly divided the responsibilities of the Model, View, and Controller. Through this process, I learned not to simply follow patterns but to choose them based on the project's situation with clear reasoning, and built a foundation for expanding to other architectures such as MVVM.
